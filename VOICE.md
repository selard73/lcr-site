# Voice & social-media guide — Last Chance Ranch of South Carolina

How the Ranch sounds, on the website and on Facebook. Written for Shannon, and
for whoever posts next. Companion to [HANDOVER.md](HANDOVER.md) (accounts,
continuity) and [CONTENT-TODO.md](CONTENT-TODO.md) (facts still to confirm).

## The conceit

Last Chance Ranch is the **Department of Pawctions**: a correctional facility
whose inmates are dogs and cats doing time for circumstances beyond their
control. The Warden narrates. The joke is that a small volunteer rescue is run
with the deadpan bureaucracy of a state penitentiary — booking numbers, rap
sheets, official stamps, parole hearings.

The vocabulary, used consistently everywhere:

| Prison word | Means |
|---|---|
| Inmate | A dog or cat at the Ranch |
| Apply for parole | Adopt |
| Halfway house | Foster |
| Yard crew | Volunteers |
| Stock the commissary | Donate |
| Rap sheet / inmate file | The animal's profile |
| The Warden | The rescue's voice (also the actual staff) |
| Serving time for circumstances beyond their control | Why they're here |
| All inmates innocent | Sign-off, everywhere |

**Retired:** "Post Bail." It reads as *adopt* to half the audience and *donate*
to the other half. Money asks say **Donate**, plainly.

## The five rules that matter most

Every one of these came from a post that missed before it landed.

**1. The dog is the joke — never the reader.**
The humor is always at the expense of the animal's dignity, never the
audience's intelligence or generosity. Even the sincere posts (seniors,
medical cases, pleas) stay in character. A straight brochure-voice post reads
as a different account and gets ignored.

**2. Never write a joke that makes a dog sound unfriendly.**
Every line is, functionally, an adoption ad. "I don't share" is funny in
isolation and reads as *resource guarding* to an adopter. Grumpy about
bath time, yes. Anything a nervous adopter could read as a warning, no.

**3. Every punchline must land without explanation.**
If it needs a beat of setup to make sense — an inside reference, a pun that
requires knowing the medication — cut it. The reader is scrolling.

**4. No links in the post body. Ever.**
Facebook suppresses posts that send people off-platform. The body ends with
"links in the comments 👇" and the link goes in a comment that gets pinned.
The pinned comment **always** includes the dog's file on the records app;
the quiz link works as a next-day reply to bump the post.

**5. Money asks are dog-first, in the dogs' voices.**
"Sponsor a dog, tiers start at $10" is a product ad and it flops. "Dear
Sponsor, I would like one (1) tennis ball. I have three under my bed but
those are evidence. — Blue, #34" is the same ask and it works. Lead with the
animal wanting something; the program is the fine print.

## Register

Deadpan officialese, warmed by obvious affection. The Warden writes like a
civil servant who loves these animals very much and will not admit it in
writing.

- Short declaratives. Bureaucratic nouns doing silly work.
- "The Warden allowed it. Look at them."
- Charges are specific and absurd: *Aggravated Cuddling*, *Tampering with a
  Full Water Glass*, *Impersonating a Liquid*, *Obstruction of a Food Bowl*.
- Understate the sweet parts. Never tell the reader how to feel.
- Emoji: sparingly, as punctuation — 🚨 ⚖️ 📋 👇. Not decoration.

**Species matters.** Cats knock things off counters, ignore direct orders,
and judge houseguests. Dogs have zoomies, steal sandwiches, and greet doors
excessively. Don't charge a cat with squirrel-related flight risk.

## Facts — get these right

- A **sanctuary**, not a shelter. Their own line: animals are not stacked in
  cages waiting for a deadline. They live there — sofas, bedrooms, yards.
- **No-kill**, volunteer-run, registered **501(c)(3)**, ~**4 acres** in
  Aiken, SC. No salaries, no overhead.
- **NOT every animal is pulled from a euthanasia list.** Some were; the
  blanket claim appears in older marketing copy and is not true. Safe
  phrasing: *"serving time for circumstances beyond their control."*
- Full vetting before adoption. Applications are reviewed carefully,
  references checked, and sometimes the answer is no — "we make no
  apologies for that."
- Donations: Cash App **$LastChanceRanchSC** · **paypal.me/LastChanceRanchSC**
- Tagline: *Saving lives one inmate at a time.*

## What works on their Facebook page

Learned the hard way on a page with 4,000+ followers.

- **Story videos are the engine.** The Cold Case Files episodes (a dog's
  absurd backstory as a dramatic reenactment) pull 1,000+ views and real
  comments. Everything else rides on their coattails.
- **Reels get distribution; link posts get buried.** If it matters, it's a
  reel.
- **Product-shaped posts flop.** A post announcing a tool or a program,
  however clever, underperforms a post about one dog. When promoting a
  feature, shape it as a dog story — a roundup of who got booked this week,
  not an ad for the booking booth.
- **Cadence:** 3–4 posts a week, maximum. The Saturday episode is the floor.
  More than four and the good ones compete with each other.
- **Vary the look between reels.** Two reels in the same visual style read as
  the same post; people scroll past the second one.
- Shannon posts the pinned comment manually at post time.
