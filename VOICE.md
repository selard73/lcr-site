# Voice brief — Last Chance Ranch of South Carolina

Reference for anyone (human or AI) writing social media and website copy for
this rescue. Self-contained: everything needed is in this file.

---

## 1. Who this is

Last Chance Ranch of South Carolina is a small, no-kill, volunteer-run dog and
cat **sanctuary** on about four acres in Aiken, South Carolina. Registered
501(c)(3). No salaries, no overhead. Animals that never find a home stay for
life — that is the point of the place.

It presents itself as the **Department of Pawctions**: a correctional facility
whose inmates are dogs and cats "serving time for circumstances beyond their
control." The Warden narrates. The joke is that a tiny rescue is described with
the deadpan bureaucracy of a state penitentiary — booking numbers, rap sheets,
official stamps, parole hearings.

Primary channel: Facebook, ~4,000 followers. Also a website
(lastchanceranchsc.com) and a public dog-records app.

---

## 2. Fixed vocabulary — use these exact terms

Do not introduce synonyms. The consistency is most of what makes the conceit
work.

| Term | Means |
|---|---|
| Inmate | A dog or cat at the Ranch |
| Apply for parole | Adopt |
| Halfway house | Foster |
| Yard crew | Volunteers |
| Stock the commissary | Donate |
| Rap sheet / inmate file | An animal's profile |
| The Warden | The rescue's voice, and its actual staff |
| Maximum Security Snuggles Unit | The collective of animals/supporters |
| All inmates innocent | Standard sign-off |

**Retired term — never use:** "Post bail." Readers split on whether it meant
*adopt* or *donate*. Money asks say **Donate**, plainly.

---

## 3. Voice

Deadpan officialese, warmed by obvious affection. The Warden writes like a
civil servant who loves these animals very much and will not put that in
writing.

- Short declarative sentences. Bureaucratic nouns doing silly work.
- Understate the sweet parts. Never instruct the reader how to feel. No
  "heartwarming," no "tears in our eyes," no "share if you agree."
- Specific beats clever. Absurd-but-official beats zany.
- Emoji as punctuation, not decoration: 🚨 ⚖️ 📋 👇 and little else.
- No hashtag stacks. `#GetBooked` and the occasional single tag, that's it.

**Calibration example (this is the target):**

> 🚨 BOOKING REPORT — DEPARTMENT OF PAWCTIONS
>
> The booking desk processed two suspects today: PRUDENCE & PAXTON, charged as
> co-conspirators with EXCESSIVE ZOOMIES. They refused to be booked separately.
> The Warden allowed it. Look at them.

**Charges** are the house style in miniature — official-sounding and absurd:
Aggravated Cuddling · Grand Theft Sandwich · Excessive Zoomies · Loitering on
Furniture · Obstruction of a Food Bowl · Impersonating a Very Good Boy ·
Flight Risk (Squirrel-Adjacent) · Tampering with a Full Water Glass ·
Impersonating a Liquid · Excessive Judgment of Houseguests · Aggravated
Panhandling (Was Already Fed) · Ankle Assault.

**Species matters.** Dogs: zoomies, stolen sandwiches, excessive door
greetings, shoe destruction. Cats: knocking things off counters, ignoring
direct orders, sitting on important documents, judging houseguests. Never
charge a cat with anything squirrel-related.

## 4. Hard rules

Ordered by how much damage getting them wrong does. Each came from a post that
missed before one landed.

**1. The dog is the joke, never the reader.** Humor comes at the expense of an
animal's dignity — never the audience's intelligence or generosity. Even
serious posts (seniors, medical cases, urgent pleas) stay in character. A
sincere brochure-voice post reads as a different account and gets scrolled
past.

**2. Never write a joke that makes an animal sound unfriendly.** Every line is
functionally an adoption ad. "Grumpy about bath time" is funny. A punchline
about a dog not sharing is a laugh on Facebook and *resource guarding* to
someone deciding whether to apply. Nothing that could read as a warning.

**3. Every punchline must land without explanation.** If it needs a beat of
setup — an inside reference, a pun that depends on knowing what a medication
is for — cut it.

**4. Never put a link in the post body.** Facebook suppresses posts that send
people off-platform. End the body with "links in the comments 👇" and supply
the link separately as a pinned comment. The pinned comment always includes
the specific animal's file; a second link works better as a next-day reply
that bumps the post.

**5. Money asks are dog-first, in the animal's own voice.** Program-shaped
asks flop; the same ask spoken by the animal works.

- ✗ *"Sponsor an inmate! Tiers start at just $10/month and support food,
  medical care, and enrichment."*
- ✓ *"Dear Sponsor, I would like one (1) tennis ball. I have three under my
  bed but those are evidence. — Blue, #34"*

**6. Never invent facts about a specific animal.** Names, inmate numbers, ages,
breeds, medical history, and backstory come from the rescue's records. If a
detail isn't supplied, write around it or leave a clearly marked blank for the
rescue to fill. Invented biography about a real adoptable animal is the worst
possible error here.

**7. Never commit the rescue to anything unconfirmed** — events, deadlines,
matched-donation promises, "we'll send you monthly updates" — unless it has
been confirmed by the rescue.

## 5. Facts to get right

- **Sanctuary, not shelter.** Their own words: the animals aren't stacked in
  cages waiting on a deadline. They live there — sofas, bedrooms, yards, cat
  towers. Most spend far more time out than in.
- **NOT every animal was pulled from a euthanasia list.** Some were. The
  blanket claim appears in older marketing copy, including the rescue's own
  listings on third-party sites, and repeating it is inaccurate. Correct
  phrasing: *"serving time for circumstances beyond their control."*
- Every animal is fully vetted before adoption, including spay/neuter.
- Adoption is deliberate: applications reviewed carefully, references checked,
  homes vetted. Sometimes it takes time and sometimes the answer is no — "we
  make no apologies for that."
- They are looking for **forever homes**, not temporary placements.
- Donations: Cash App **$LastChanceRanchSC** · **paypal.me/LastChanceRanchSC**
- Tagline: *Saving lives one inmate at a time.*
- Three things keep the gates open: **volunteers, donations, and paroles.**

---

## 6. Channel mechanics

- **Story videos are the engine.** The *Cold Case Files* series — an animal's
  absurd criminal backstory as a dramatic reenactment — carries the account.
  Episode 1 (Isaiah, contractor fraud) drew 1,000+ views and double-digit
  comments in five hours. A polished post announcing a free tool, same page,
  same week, drew almost nothing.
- **Reels get distribution; link posts get buried.** If it matters, it's a
  reel.
- **Product-shaped posts flop.** To promote a feature or program, shape it as
  an animal story — a roundup of who got booked this week, not an ad for the
  booking booth.
- **Cadence: 3–4 posts per week maximum**, with one story episode as the
  floor. Past four, good posts compete with each other.
- **Vary the visual style between reels.** Two in the same treatment read as
  the same post and the second gets scrolled.

---

## 7. What a finished post looks like

Deliver both parts:

1. **Post body** — in voice, no links, ending with "links in the comments 👇"
   when a link is relevant.
2. **Pinned comment** — the animal's file link, plus adopt/foster/donate links
   as appropriate.

Recurring formats that work:

- **Booking report** — a new intake or a newly pinned mugshot, written as a
  processing record.
- **Inmate File Friday** — one animal's rap sheet: charges, warden's notes,
  conditions of parole.
- **Cold Case Files** — the weekly video episode, an absurd backstory
  reenacted; caption sets up the crime and the arrest.
- **Parole granted** — an adoption announcement, written as a release from
  custody.
